Approach

## Four phases, in order

Here’s how I take a design task from a blank page to a shippable UI.

Understand the client’s requirements, the target audience and the market, through interviews, surveys and research. **Here:** requirement analysis and competitor analysis.

Define goals, objectives and design strategy with stakeholders, aligning the product vision and key features. **Here:** personas and problem statements.

Organize and structure the content for intuitive navigation, using wireframes and prototypes to set the hierarchy. **Here:** feature prioritization and information architecture.

Create on-brand high-fidelity mockups and interactive prototypes, then iterate on feedback and usability testing. **Here:** UI design and design system.

Phase 1 · Understand

## Requirement analysis

Who are the target users?

Mobile job seekers at every level, from entry-level to executive. Usually looking for a new job, sometimes researching careers or companies.

What are their needs and pain points?

Finding jobs that fit their skills, and applying quickly. The biggest pain is time: hours on job boards and filling in online applications.

Which features are a must?

- Search by location, title and other criteria
- Job details: description, salary range, company info
- Apply online or with a résumé
- Save jobs for later
- Track applications

What’s the user journey?

Create profile Search View details Apply Track status

What does the market look like?

Crowded, with Indeed, LinkedIn and Glassdoor holding large user bases. The trend is mobile-first, so the app has to work beautifully on a small screen.

Phase 2 · Define

## Five people looking for work

General personas to start with, narrowed down later to match each iteration’s requirements. Tap a card to see what stands in their way.

### Sarah, the professional

Find a job that fits her career goals, see complete listings, grow her network and simplify applying.

### Challenges

Sorting through too many listings, little insider information about companies, and networking is hard.

### John, the new graduate

Land an entry-level job in his field, gain experience and explore career paths.

### Challenges

A small network, no industry connections, and entry-level roles are hard to find.

### The career switcher

Advance her career, move to a new industry, and find senior roles that match her expertise.

### Challenges

Navigating a change of field, matching her skills to new roles, and building a network in a new industry.

### The freelancer

Find short-term projects and gigs, and connect with clients who need his skills.

### Challenges

Few full-time options, a flexible schedule to manage, and an inconsistent income.

### Lisa, the intern

Gain practical experience through internships or apprenticeships and build industry skills.

### Challenges

Limited experience, competing with stronger candidates, and finding room to grow.

Phase 2 · Define

## The problem, in 5W1H

Job platforms lack personalization, deep company insights, real networking and good application management, so job seekers struggle to find the right opportunities.

Job seekers, wading through huge numbers of listings with little company information, few connections and messy applications.

Across the job search app industry, where current platforms fail to offer a seamless, user-centered experience.

Right now. People feel frustrated and inefficient during their search because of these gaps.

Missing personalization, insights, networking and application tools lower satisfaction and make it harder to land the job they want.

An app with personal recommendations, deep company insights, strong networking and efficient application management that closes the gap between people and the jobs they want.

Phase 3 · Conceive

## Red routes first

The red route technique ranks features by the critical paths users take most. It keeps the work user-centered, spends limited resources on what matters, validates the core early and gets a useful product to launch sooner.

**Foundation and core features.** The scope of this task.

### Registration & profile

Name, contact details and experience, so the search can be personal.

### Job listings

A clear list with filters for location, industry and experience level.

### Advanced filters

Salary range, job type and company size to narrow results down.

**Personas served:** Sarah (profile and saved jobs), John (entry-level listings and filters) and Lisa (internships through search and filters).

**Personalization and connection.**

### Networking

Profiles, connections, messaging, industry communities and forums.

### Recommendations

AI suggestions from preferences, behavior and saved searches.

### Company insights

Culture, employee reviews, benefits, values and growth.

**Streamlining the application process.**

### Résumé builder

Custom templates for skills, experience and education.

### Application tracking

Status, reminders for interviews and deadlines.

### Interview prep

Tips, sample questions and best practices.

## Information architecture

Focused on iteration 1’s critical paths: search, read a listing, apply, and keep track.

**Home** 3

- Personal recommendations
- Quick search bar
- Browse by category

**Job details** 3

- Title, company, location, salary, responsibilities
- Apply in the app
- Save for later

**Search & filters** 3

- Location, salary, type, experience
- Sort by relevance or date
- Clear preference input

**Profile** 3

- Preferences and contact info
- Résumé upload
- Saved jobs and history

**Notifications** 2

- New jobs and status updates
- Custom frequency and types

**Settings** 2

- Account, privacy, notifications
- Help, FAQs and support
